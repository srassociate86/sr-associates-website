TOPOGRAPHICAL SURVEYING

Understand the
existing terrain.

A topographical survey captures the shape and features of existing ground. It records elevations and relevant natural and man-made features so that the site can be represented clearly for planning, design, engineering and development.

Depending on the project scope, survey data can be used to prepare spot levels, contours, terrain information, existing feature plans and other digital survey outputs.

01 / LEVELS

Spot Levels

Collection of elevation points across the site to describe existing ground levels and changes in terrain.

02 / CONTOURS

Contour Mapping

Contour information representing changes in ground elevation for terrain understanding and project planning.

03 / TERRAIN

Terrain Mapping

Survey data showing the existing shape and elevation characteristics of the surveyed area.

04 / FEATURES

Existing Features

Measurement and documentation of relevant natural and man-made features present at the site.

05 / SITE PLAN

Topographical Plans

Clear plan drawings combining measured features and elevation information for project use.

06 / DIGITAL

Digital Terrain Data

Organized survey data that can support CAD drawings, surfaces, terrain analysis and further processing.

GROUND & ELEVATION DATA

See the site
in detail.

Topographical surveying brings horizontal and vertical site information together. This helps represent the existing ground and important features before planning or construction begins.

  • Existing ground levels
  • Spot elevations across the site
  • Contours and terrain information
  • Roads, buildings and other existing features
  • Drainage and visible site features where required
  • Digital data for CAD and surface generation

WHAT THE SURVEY CAN SHOW

Turn ground conditions
into useful data.

Topographical survey information can be organized in different ways depending on what the project needs to understand about the existing site.

Elevation Pattern

Spot levels and contours help show how ground elevation changes across the surveyed area.

Existing Features

Important physical features can be measured and represented together with the terrain information.

Site Conditions

The survey provides measured information about existing conditions before design or development work.

Digital Surface Information

Processed survey data can support creation of digital surfaces and further CAD-based analysis.
